I made a tamagochi in GODOT

I want to develop a video game.

This was the thought process behind me joining a game jam earlier this year (2026 March).
I wanted to learn the ins and outs of making a game. I had seen some videos on gamedev stuff and I have always had an interest in making a game. 
But I finally decided to chase this dream of mine. I joined a game jam held by a YouTuber to hopefully get the grasp on what I would need to focus on making my full game.
I ended up on a team with an old friend of mine who was our artist, a programmer from India, and a writer who was from America.
I took on the role of project lead and sound engineer.

I took the role mainly to challenge myself and to help out where needed.
I have experience in all things needed to develop a game, from writing (DnD), basic pixel art knowledge, a basic understanding of the engine, and I make music as a hobby.
Sure it's not perfect but nothing needs to be. The team worked together pretty alright. 
Before the jam started, we got to know each other and I spent some time in meetings with all team members to get to know them and also understand their strengths and weaknesses.
After talking to all of them I had doubts about our writer, but I decided to give him a chance. After the jam began the writer was an anchor for the team and couldn't handle delivering any work on time or even what I asked.
So I decided to talk to him, gave him another chance, told him it was his last, and he ended up handing me an AI-generated document. So I asked the other team members and we decided to let him go.

This meant I had to pick up more slack and work as the writer as well for our game.
This went alright in my own opinion. Due to a flu both me and the programmer were knocked out for about a week. 
This caused the project to fail due to a lack of time.

I genuinely am quite sad we couldn't finish the platformer we had talked about. 
That said, I am extremely proud of my team for pulling together after losing 2 weeks of development time,
and mainly making the project next to schooling or other tasks.

This entire story aside, the experience in managing a team was stressful in its own way.
I didn't really get the chance to be hands-on with programming. So I decided to make a little project in Godot.
I have made Tamagotchis in many programming languages and methods, from a physical one for a school assignment with Arduino,
to different versions in Python and JavaScript. Why? Well, because a Tamagotchi is in my opinion a simple but also fun way to learn a new language/framework.
So to learn the basics in Godot and GDScript I decided to make one! I spent about 9 hours total on making this Tamagotchi without the help of genAI because I kind of want to make projects without it.

It was easier than I expected to learn how to use Godot and the nodes they use.
I genuinely hope to make further improvements or better variations of this in the future.
But with a good feeling and trust in myself knowing Godot, I will now go further on the mountain of gamedevelopment and hopefully climb to the top and launch my own game.
But for now I have the feeling that no matter what I do, the range and height of my ambitions will be reached eventually,
but I personally hope sooner rather than later.
